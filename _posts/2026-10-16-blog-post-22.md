Preface
======

Product management is a broad discipline. It combines customer understanding, business strategy, data analysis, technical knowledge, communication, and execution. Becoming a better Product Manager requires more than learning a few frameworks—it requires building a well-rounded understanding of how successful products are discovered, built, launched, and scaled.

That’s why I created **Daily PM** at [pm.alirezasn.ir](https://pm.alirezasn.ir): a structured learning resource designed to help Product Managers develop their skills one day at a time.

The website brings together a **100-day Product Management curriculum** covering everything from the fundamentals of the role to advanced topics such as AI product management, product economics, technical architecture, and organizational leadership. It also includes a growing collection of Product Management interview questions to help you prepare for your next career opportunity.

## Learn Product Management, One Day at a Time

The idea behind Daily PM is simple: make continuous learning practical and manageable.

Instead of navigating disconnected articles, videos, and frameworks, you can follow a structured learning path. Each day focuses on a specific topic, with an estimated reading time to help you plan your learning around your work and other commitments.

The curriculum progresses from foundational concepts to more advanced challenges, helping you connect individual Product Management skills into a broader understanding of the discipline.

## New Section: Product Management Interview Questions

Learning Product Management concepts is one part of professional development. Explaining your decisions, demonstrating your experience, and solving practical problems under interview conditions are equally important.

To support that journey, Daily PM now includes a dedicated **Product Management Interview Questions** section.

The questions cover different dimensions of the role, including product execution, prioritization, technical communication, analytics, stakeholder management, and product design.

Here are the first questions in the collection:

### 1. Tell me about a product you improved

A behavioral question that helps you demonstrate how you identified an opportunity, made decisions, worked with a team, and measured the results.

### 2. How do you prioritize features?

Explain how you evaluate customer value, business impact, effort, risk, and strategic alignment when deciding what a team should work on next.

### 3. Explain an API to a non-technical stakeholder

Demonstrate your ability to translate a technical concept into clear, accessible language and connect it to a business or customer outcome.

### 4. How would you improve activation for a fintech app?

Show how you would define activation, analyze the user journey, identify friction points, develop hypotheses, and validate potential improvements.

### 5. Tell me about a conflict with engineering or design

Explain how you handled competing perspectives, aligned stakeholders, and reached a decision without losing sight of the product outcome.

### 6. What metrics would you track after launching a feature?

Describe how you would define success, select meaningful outcome metrics, monitor guardrails, and use evidence to decide what to do next.

### 7. How would you decide whether to build, buy, or partner?

Demonstrate how you would compare strategic differentiation, cost, time to market, technical capabilities, operational dependencies, and long-term flexibility.

### 8. Design a product for freelancers to manage invoices

Walk through a product design problem by identifying the target users, understanding their needs, defining the core problem, exploring solutions, prioritizing an MVP, and measuring success.

These questions are a starting point for a growing interview-preparation resource. The objective is not to memorize perfect answers, but to learn how to structure your thinking, explain your reasoning, and adapt your approach to different product challenges.

## Who Is Daily PM For?

Daily PM is designed for people at different stages of their Product Management journey:

- **Aspiring Product Managers** who want a structured introduction to the discipline.
- **Junior and mid-level PMs** who want to strengthen their fundamentals and fill knowledge gaps.
- **Experienced Product Managers** who want to explore advanced topics, from product economics to AI and technical architecture.
- **Product Managers preparing for interviews** who want to practice behavioral, strategic, analytical, and product-design questions.
- **Technical professionals moving into Product Management** who want to connect their engineering knowledge with customer needs and business outcomes.

You don't need to master every topic before applying what you learn. The most effective approach is to connect the concepts to your own product, discuss them with colleagues, and practice using them to solve real problems.

## How to Get the Most Out of Daily PM

Here are a few suggestions for making the most of the curriculum:

1. **Build a consistent habit.** Set aside a small amount of time each day instead of waiting for a free weekend.
2. **Apply concepts to real products.** Think about how each framework could help you improve a product you already know.
3. **Question the frameworks.** Understand the problem each framework is designed to solve rather than applying it mechanically.
4. **Practice explaining your decisions.** Clear communication is essential to Product Management, especially when working across engineering, design, data, and business teams.
5. **Prepare for interviews through practice.** Write your own answers, use specific examples from your experience, and reflect on what you would do differently.
6. **Focus on outcomes.** Measure your progress by how your thinking and decisions improve—not only by how many lessons you complete.
